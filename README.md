# Global HIV Trends and Treatment Effectiveness Analysis

## Project Overview

This Power BI healthcare analytics project explores global HIV trends,
treatment effectiveness and differences in access to antiretroviral
therapy (ART).

The project uses an interactive Power BI dashboard to examine changes
in HIV infections and deaths over time, compare the HIV burden across
continents and investigate differences in ART coverage between countries.

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Power BI
- Data Cleaning and Transformation
- Data Visualisation
- Interactive Filters and Slicers
- KPI Development
- Healthcare Data Analysis
- Data Storytelling

## Dashboard KPIs

The dashboard tracks:

- People Living with HIV
- New HIV Infections
- HIV-related Deaths
- Average ART Coverage

## Dashboard Features

The Power BI dashboard includes:

- New HIV infections over time
- New infections compared with HIV deaths
- People living with HIV by continent
- HIV deaths by continent
- Top countries by average ART coverage
- Interactive year filter
- Continent filter
- Country filter

These filters allow users to explore the data by year,
continent and individual country.

## Key Insights

### 1. New HIV infections are declining

New HIV infections show a downward trend from the early 2000s
to 2024, suggesting progress in prevention and awareness efforts.

However, infections have not reached zero, highlighting the
continued importance of prevention, testing and awareness programmes.

### 2. HIV-related deaths have decreased

HIV-related deaths also decline over time.

This may reflect improvements in access to antiretroviral therapy,
HIV care and follow-up for people who have been diagnosed.

### 3. Africa carries the highest HIV burden

Africa has the largest number of people living with HIV and the
highest number of HIV-related deaths among the regions represented
in the dashboard.

This highlights the importance of targeted prevention, testing
and treatment interventions in high-burden regions.

### 4. ART coverage differs between countries

ART coverage varies considerably across countries.

Countries with weaker treatment coverage may require additional
support to improve access to treatment and reduce HIV-related deaths.

## Recommendations

1. Prioritise high-burden regions, particularly Africa, with
   increased support for HIV prevention, testing and ART programmes.

2. Improve ART access in countries with lower treatment coverage.

3. Strengthen prevention, testing and awareness programmes in
   areas where new HIV infections remain high.

4. Use country-level monitoring to identify gaps in treatment
   access and target resources more effectively.

## Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates that global HIV outcomes are improving,
but substantial differences remain between regions and countries.

The dashboard highlights how healthcare data can be used to identify
high-burden populations, monitor treatment access and support
data-driven decisions about where interventions and resources may
be most needed.
